Mock AI design exercise

2h

The prompt

Fin handles support over email. It has drafted an answer but confidence is low. Design the behavior for deciding whether to answer, ask the customer a clarifying question, ask a teammate for help, or escalate.

Why this exercise

This mirrors the exact reasoning pattern Burak used in the transcript when describing the diagnostic agent. The interview is likely to hand you something structurally similar — a behavior design problem with incomplete context — and watch how you reason through it.

The goal isn't to produce a polished deliverable. It's to make yourself fluent in the vocabulary and frameworks under pressure. Two hours, write it longhand or in a doc, don't over-polish.

What your 2-page response should cover

1. Situation and constraints

Email is async, so latency sensitivity is lower than chat. But cost-of-wrong-answer is higher because the customer waits longer before they can correct course. You can afford more retrieval and validation work than in synchronous channels. Name these trade-offs explicitly upfront.

2. System inputs available

What context does Fin have access to when deciding? List them:

  • Customer message and conversation history
  • Detected issue summary (from the query refinement node)
  • Retrieved passages (pre- and post-rerank)
  • Draft answer and confidence score
  • Available procedures, guidance, data connectors
  • Customer/account attributes and risk category
  • Past similar conversations

3. Decision framework

When does Fin take each path? Lay it out concretely. Examples:

  • Answer if confidence is high, answer is grounded, no policy conflict, no missing required data, low-risk action
  • Ask customer if missing customer-provided info, ambiguity is user-resolvable, no internal data required
  • Ask teammate if internal judgment needed, policy conflict, missing business context, second-best evidence could improve answer
  • Escalate if regulated/sensitive issue, destructive action, high-value customer, repeated failed attempts, model can't ground answer

4. Failure modes per component

This is where you sound like Burak rather than Eric. Separate failures by where they originate:

  • Retrieval failure: wrong source, missing source, stale source, policy conflict
  • Reranking failure: correct passage retrieved but ranked too low
  • Generation failure: correct evidence, bad synthesis
  • Decision failure: should have escalated but answered (or vice versa)
  • Validation failure: unsafe or ungrounded answer passed checks
  • UX failure: teammate gets a vague ask, can't respond usefully

5. Eval rubric

Component-level evals (so you can isolate where failures originate):

  • Retrieval accuracy
  • Reranking relevance
  • Tool choice correctness
  • Handoff decision accuracy
  • Answer groundedness

End-to-end evals (so you can measure the user-visible outcome):

  • Resolution rate
  • False automation rate (Fin answered when it should have escalated)
  • False escalation rate (Fin escalated when it could have answered)
  • Teammate usefulness when asked
  • Customer effort
  • Cost per resolved conversation
  • Latency

6. Trade-offs across Pedro's 8 dimensions

Name at least 3 trade-offs you're consciously making. Examples:

  • Choosing higher escalation rate at the cost of resolution rate, to protect against hallucinations on regulated topics
  • Accepting higher cost-per-conversation by running extra validation, to reduce harmful hallucinations
  • Optimising for hard-resolution rate over total resolution, because email customers will revisit a bad answer

7. 3–5 design principles

Explicit, defensible statements. Examples:

  • "Prefer asking the customer over escalating to a teammate when the missing information is user-provided, not internal"
  • "Surface the source and applied rule when Fin asks a teammate, never the raw chain-of-thought"
  • "Default to escalation when confidence score is uncalibrated for the risk class of the conversation"

How to reason during the exercise

Match Burak's pattern from the transcript:

  1. Separate failures by component before proposing fixes
  2. Ask what evidence is available before assuming the model is wrong
  3. Don't reach for "make the model think longer" without knowing what it's reasoning over
  4. Treat confidence scores as suspect until calibration is verified
  5. Treat human handoff as a product behavior to design, not an error state to minimize

What to do with the output

Keep this doc. Bring it to the interview as a portfolio artifact. Even if they don't ask for it directly, mentioning "I drafted my approach to a Fin behavior problem — happy to share" is a real signal of preparation.
